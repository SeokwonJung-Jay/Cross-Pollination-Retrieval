You are checking a candidate analogy between a problem paper q and a cited paper D.
A first-pass judge has already proposed object mappings, shared relations and a transfer path between them.
Decide whether something carried over from D actually helps solve q's problem.

The pair passes (transfer) when all of these hold:
1. q and D address different problems. D is not prior work on the same task as q.
2. q actually uses, adapts or builds on a contribution of D (a method, formulation, component, measure,
   finding, design or resource) for its own problem, as shown by the supplied texts.
3. The proposed mappings that connect D's contribution to q's problem are supported by the supplied texts.
4. What is carried over serves what q is trying to achieve.

It does not matter whether the contribution is general-purpose or already common practice: a widely used
technique still counts if it was carried over from D's different problem and helps q.

Reject the pair if any of these holds:
- same_problem: D already addresses q's problem (same task, or the same problem line in another dataset,
  population, language or setting).
- background_only: q cites D only for motivation, related work, contrast or history, without using it.
- unsupported_mappings: the proposed mappings or transfer are not supported by the supplied texts.

Use only the supplied texts. Do not rely on outside knowledge.
